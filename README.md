# Brand_Audit_Olipop
Brand Audit Written Report and Slide Deck for Strategic Brand Management class

Strategic Brand Management-1 (ADV 2342) – Fall 2024
Brand Audit (75 points)

Assignment: You are Brand X’s new Strategic Brand Manager. You have been tasked
with conducting a brand audit to decide on Brand X’s current market health and suggest
ways to improve and leverage its equity for the future.

Following the steps of the brand audit (refer to Rolex Product Portfolio, p. 322-328),
please answer the following questions. Use an essay format and apply sub-headings
where relevant to highlight major sections (e.g., Brand Inventory, Brand Exploratory,
etc.).

1. Introduction: [2.5 points]
a. Provide the brand history and describe their target market.
  i. Evaluate past records of the brand, i.e., past marketing activities,
  blogs, and newspaper/magazine articles on the brand.
b. Delve into their product portfolio of offerings. See if you can
compartmentalize them into unique product categories (see Figure 9-7, p.
323).
   
2. Brand Inventory: [15 points]
a. Products – What makes the products different or unique from the
competition? Dig deep into the attributes and benefits associated with the
brand’s products. I encourage you to sample a few of their products to get a
first-hand experience.
b. Brand elements – Which brand elements does the brand emphasize on? Which
is the most prominent? How and where are they utilized? Are they all
consistent or are there different versions for no obvious reasons? Give
reasons.
c. Pricing – Is the brand high-end? What is the range of prices for their products?
Do they charge a premium? Do you see a value offering, meaning, will
consumers be interested in paying a premium for the product? Is the pricing
associated or aligned with their brand image and target market? Give reasons.
d. Distribution – What channels does the brand use to distribute their products
(direct, indirect, or online)? Provide rationale as to the appropriateness of the
current channels (think of target market).
e. Marketing and communication strategy – Look at recent campaigns and
describe the different communication strategies that were utilized to promote
the brand – advertising, brand ambassadors, sponsorships, philanthropy, etc.
Are there any secondary brand entities (Ch-8) that the brand associates itself
with? If yes, please elaborate.
f. Provide a summary of key points.

3. Brand Exploratory: [30 points]
a. Consumer knowledge – Briefly interview 25 family and/or friends (not team
member) and explore their perceptions of the brand (awareness and image).
Look for associations that can help you build a mental map. You would then
have to categorize them into core brand associations and label them
accordingly. The categories would give a sense of the brand’s POPs and
PODs (see Figure 9-9, Rolex Mental Map, p. 326).
b. Brand resonance pyramid – Briefly apply the brand resonance model and see
where the brand stands. Specifically, request the same sample of
family/friends (n = 25) to fill out the brand tracking survey and on analyzing
the data, evaluate which components the brand is doing well or not well on
and where should improvements be made?
c. Competition – Who is the competition [select a total of two direct
competitors]? Elaborate on each competitor and explain on what aspects
(POPs and PODs) do they compete with the brand. Furthermore, compare
each brand’s mantra (i.e., brand vs. competitors).
d. Provide a summary of key points.

4. Recommendations: [10 points]
Based on the information gathered in the brand ‘exploratory,’ propose
recommendations to the Chief Marketing Officer (CMO):
a. Strategic (positioning):
  i. Based on the POPs and PODs – how do you think the brand should
  position itself in the future?
  • Hint: Will the new positioning help overcome the weaknesses
  (if any) found in the brand resonance pyramid? Elaborate
  accordingly.
  ii. With the new positioning, provide a ‘new’ brand mantra.
  • Hint: Use the already created mental maps to develop a new
  brand mantra that captures the core values and attributes of the
  brand.
b. Tactical:
  i. Think of three (or more) possible opportunities that can be
  implemented to help the brand enhance its equity. Elaborate on each
  opportunity.

5. Conclusion: [2.5 points]
a. Summarize and highlight the main points of the brand audit.

6. Presentation: [10 points]
a. The PowerPoint presentation should NOT go over 15 minutes, while 5
minutes will be devoted to Q&A. ALL team members should present and each
member’s presentation will be evaluated. The brand audit proposal and
presentation slides should be virtually submitted to the instructor on the day of
the presentation.

7. References: [5 points]
Minimum of TEN external sources to support your viewpoints should be used and
have to be cited in APA style. Use relevant journal articles (e.g., Journal of
Advertising), trade publications (e.g., Advertising Age), and consumer reports (e.g.,
Statista, Simmons Insights, Mintel). Your textbook and the brand website will NOT
be counted as unique citations.
• Please refrain from using corporate websites, blogs, or articles from websites that
are unknown. It would diminish the credibility of your arguments. Good examples
include: trade publications (e.g., Ad Age, AdWeek, Forbes), journal articles (e.g.,
Journal of Advertising, International Journal of Advertising), Nielsen study
reports, etc.
• Do NOT use quotes. Instead, paraphrase, as I would like to see more of your
opinions/thinking in the writing.
• Do not use sources that have missing author names, dates, title, etc. because it just
makes it difficult to cite them in APA style.
• Visit APA for specific instructions on citations: https://apastyle.apa.org/style-
grammar-guidelines/references/examples
• Meet with Hamon Arts Librarian Naomi Schemm (nschemm@smu.edu) for
further guidance.
• Scholastic dishonesty harms everyone and policies on scholastic dishonesty will
be strictly enforced. Plagiarism, as defined in Webster’s Dictionary is: “to take
(ideas, writings, etc.) from (another) and pass them off as one’s own.” Therefore,
handing in a paper written by someone else is a clear example of plagiarism or
using work of another student. Even the act of writing one sentence in your
paper word-for-word of what someone else has written is also a form of
plagiarism, unless you credit the author and put the statement in quotation marks.
Information taken from the Internet is no exception to the rule. In general,
always try to paraphrase (write in your own words) the ideas of authors and
be sure to cite their names within the body of your work. Be careful and if you
are unsure of something, please come in and see me.
